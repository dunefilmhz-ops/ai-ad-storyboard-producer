# Case Lessons

These are general failure patterns extracted from real projects. Do not reuse their characters, settings or plots.

## Identity and design accuracy

- Facial hair, forehead marks, helmet shape and other small identity features trigger revisions when they diverge from the character sheet.
- Close-ups require stricter identity fidelity than wide shots.
- A character can look acceptable in one frame but drift across shot size; compare a contact sheet, not isolated frames.

## Light and effects

- The light source must remain physically consistent across adjacent shots. A glow originating inside cannot move outside without explanation.
- Effects need source, movement and interaction. Static underwater creatures, frozen gunfire and inert magic look artificial.
- Projectile trails should match the requested brightness, length, speed and direction; post-production is preferable when generation cannot control them.

## Spatial continuity

- Ship, flight, walk and attack direction must connect across cuts.
- Door opening needs a motivated prior action such as a kick or push.
- Characters must walk or fly fully out of frame when the next shot depends on that exit.
- Reverse shots with nearly identical background and shot size feel like broken repeats; vary framing while preserving geography.

## Action and impact

- Shooting requires body recoil, weapon movement, camera response and a readable projectile cue.
- A victory needs a visible defeated state and a winner reaction.
- Transformations need trigger, process and completed form; use a bridge such as burst, smoke, light or debris.

## Composition and prop continuity

- Avoid accidental symmetry when the scene should feel dynamic.
- Match container/prop counts and states across shots; remove objects that disappear in the next shot.
- Confirm orientation and open/closed angles of doors, lids and mechanical parts.

## Technical and editorial

- Remove accidental watermarks from every frame.
- Fix overexposure, blur and frame drops before detailed cosmetic feedback.
- When two cuts feel unnatural, a longer continuation of the stronger shot can be better than keeping both.
- Use post for exact bullets, UI, text, glow polish and transitions when AI generation is unreliable.

## Generalization rule

Translate every client comment into a reusable category: identity, state, geography, action, effect, timing, camera, technical quality or delivery. Store the category and acceptance test, not the case-specific noun.
