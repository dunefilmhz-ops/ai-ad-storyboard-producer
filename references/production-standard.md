# Production Standard

## Source hierarchy

Treat the current client's formal art-production standard as mandatory. Treat scripts and feedback PDFs as project evidence, not universal story templates.

## Required gates

1. Receive and normalize the requirement package.
2. Create character/style/environment anchors.
3. Return image storyboards or keyframes for approval.
4. Generate the first video version only after keyframe approval.
5. Collect feedback by shot number and revise major viewing problems first.
6. Complete edit, grade, restoration, interpolation, sound, effects and packaging.
7. Run final technical and visual QA before delivery.

## Minimum input package

Use what is available and flag what is missing:

- story script and special camera references;
- character sheets or identity references;
- art-style reference;
- environment reference;
- game UI, gameplay, logo, font, code and end-card assets;
- ratio, resolution, fps, duration and platform.

Pure-text scripts are valid. Build provisional anchors and mark them as assumptions when client art is unavailable.

## Keyframe approval checks

- art style matches the requirement;
- character identity and costume state are accurate;
- environment and light source are coherent;
- composition attracts attention and reserves text-safe areas;
- action instant and spatial relationship are readable;
- props, hands and contact points are plausible.

## First-video checks

- content corresponds to the approved production shot;
- character, costume and prop states remain stable;
- action has anticipation, execution and result;
- adjacent shots connect in direction, position, scale and light;
- emotion and pace support the advertising beat;
- no obvious continuity break, watermark or AI anomaly.

## Revision priorities

Fix high-impact issues first: wrong identity, wrong direction, broken action, unexplained light, scene discontinuity, severe deformation, unreadable selling point and wrong delivery specs. Avoid spending disproportionate time on invisible micro-defects while major viewing problems remain.

## Post-production is required

Use post-production for rhythm, shot order, unified grading, restoration, sharpening, frame-rate normalization, sound, BGM, transitions, screen shake, flashes, speed ramps, exact text, UI, logos, store badges and local repairs.

Ask generation models for environment sound and effects only when audio is useful; keep BGM separate for editorial control.

## Delivery package

- final video in every requested aspect ratio;
- clean version without logo/end card when requested;
- editable project or agreed interchange files;
- prompts for all AI images and videos;
- original generated image and video segments;
- exact text/UI assets and font information;
- version map and shot-level revision record.

## Technical QA

Verify with media metadata, not visual guesswork:

- width, height and aspect ratio;
- resolution and codec;
- fps consistency;
- duration;
- audio presence and format;
- no unexpected stream, watermark or corrupted frame.

Never mark complete when a required 9:16 delivery is 16:9, even if the story is correct.
